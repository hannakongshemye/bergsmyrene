The farm

A market garden run biodynamically since 1981

Bergsmyrene produces organic, biodynamic vegetables in Hurum. Everything grown on the farm is sold locally, to families, shops and restaurants.

What is here

On the farm we have a shop where you can buy our produce all year round. Through the summer we also have a flower garden you can pick from yourself, and a village store where we sell produce from other farms along with general organic staples. On top of that we have a small bakery with very good sourdough bread. Check the opening hours to find out when there is bread.

We make a point of growing a broad range of common vegetables, but also greenhouse crops, herbs, berries and wild plants. In the high season, from July to September, we have nearly everything you could wish for in vegetables, plus a few you did not know you wanted. Do get in touch if you want something you cannot find in Norwegian shops. We will see what we can do.

If you want something we grow but cannot get out to the farm, you can order it here on the site and have it delivered to your home or to a pickup point in Oslo and Drammen. Delivery is every Thursday.

Philosophy and method

Everything is only resources

Our aim is to make genuinely sustainable, healthy and flavourful food. We achieve that by letting the biological processes steer the work we do on the farm. Fundamentally that means we only use natural materials to create the conditions our plants need to grow large and fine. And we would rather find those natural materials here on the farm.

That is one reason we keep animals. The animals turn materials we cannot use ourselves into first-class plant nutrition. Their manure is also an important part of the composting we do. Into the compost we can add surplus from production, prunings and much more of what would otherwise be waste. In a natural system there is no waste; everything is only resources.

That same biology happens to be exactly what plants need around their roots in order to take up nutrition from the soil in a natural way. By spreading compost we add nutrition and microbial life, which creates the conditions our plants need to grow large, strong and full of flavour. Then we eat the plants and put what we do not eat back into the compost, and there we have a fine circle going. A circle that, if we tend it well, can carry on forever. That is what we call sustainability.

We also try to minimise our footprint by limiting the use of machinery and fossil fuel where we can. We avoid plastic packaging in both trade and private sales, and we give customers the option of loose weight in the farm shop.

History

From Finn to Severin

Bergsmyrene has been farmed biodynamically and organically since 1981, when Finn bought the farm and converted it to biodynamic methods. A few years later Evelyn Romer Iversen joined, and until 2022 the two of them ran the farm together. They had many irons in the fire over the years: many years of dairy production, later laying hens, and a range of animals and poultry including horses, sheep, goats, pigs, peacocks and geese. They also ran social projects, green care for people with functional variations, and school on the farm in various formats. The hen house was converted into a school kitchen, where we now cook for the farm's many trainees. In the beginning they also worked the soil with horses. And of course there has been vegetable production the whole time.

In 2022 Severin officially bought the farm from Finn and Evelyn. That is how it works, even when it passes on within a family. Severin and Emma now officially run the farm. Beyond the formal ownership, not much has changed. We still work together in the same constellation as before. But Finn and Evelyn are slowly withdrawing, and Severin and Emma are starting their own projects. So far the bakery, the village store and the online shop are the biggest changes. Production carries on as before.

The people

Who is here

The farm is run by us, plus many willing hands who come and help for shorter spells in exchange for knowledge, board and lodging. We have had people from all over the world here over the years, and in the high season a great many want to come to us for adventure, a different kind of holiday, or to learn. The farm then turns into a teeming, living, cheerful camp with many languages and a great deal of life and bustle.
